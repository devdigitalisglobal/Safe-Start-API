## Regular checks — every few weeks
- Check tyre pressures against the manufacturer's recommended pressure
- Inspect tyres for damage, cracks and uneven wear
- Check tyre tread depth
- Check the engine oil level
- Check coolant level when the engine is cool
- Check windscreen washer fluid
- Check headlights, brake lights and indicators
- Check windscreen wipers and washer operation
- Look underneath the car for signs of fluid leaks
- Pay attention to any new dashboard warning lights

## Before a long trip
- Check all tyre pressures, including the spare if fitted
- Check tyre condition and tread
- Check oil and coolant levels
- Fill windscreen washer fluid
- Check lights and indicators
- Make sure you have enough fuel or EV charge for the journey
- Check that your spare tyre or puncture repair kit is usable
- Make sure you know your roadside assistance details

## Tyres
- Know where to find your car's recommended tyre pressures
- Never assume the pressure shown on the tyre sidewall is the vehicle's recommended operating pressure
- Check for uneven wear, which may warrant further investigation
- Replace damaged or excessively worn tyres
- Have wheel alignment/balance checked if you notice unusual wear or vibration

## Dashboard warning lights
- Learn what the major warning lights mean
- Don't ignore a warning light because the car still seems to drive normally
- Check the owner's manual if an unfamiliar warning appears
- Treat red warnings seriously and respond according to the manufacturer's instructions
- Have persistent warning lights professionally investigated

## Brakes
- Pay attention to squealing, grinding or unusual brake noises
- Have brakes checked if the pedal feels different from normal
- Investigate vibration when braking
- Don't delay investigating a suspected braking problem

## Battery
- Watch for slow or difficult starting
- Have the battery checked if starting becomes sluggish
- Check for battery warning lights
- If fitted with a conventional accessible battery, check for obvious terminal corrosion without touching damaged or leaking components
- Consider battery condition before a long trip, particularly with an older battery

## Servicing
- Follow the manufacturer's recommended service schedule
- Don't wait for something to break before servicing the car
- Keep service invoices and maintenance records
- Check when the next service is due
- Address issues identified during servicing rather than simply ignoring them

## Know when something isn't right
Have the vehicle checked if you notice:
- New or unusual noises
- Steering pulling to one side
- New vibration through the steering wheel or vehicle
- Braking feels different
- Engine overheating
- Smoke or unusual smells
- Fluid underneath the vehicle
- Difficulty starting
- Loss of power
- A warning light that remains illuminated

## Keep your car road-ready
- Registration current
- Insurance current
- Driver's licence current
- Roadworthy/safety inspection completed when required
- Number plates secure and visible
- Windscreen and mirrors provide clear visibility
- Seatbelts working correctly
- Roadside assistance details accessible
